Methodology

AIECO estimates the energy and carbon footprint of AI usage from peer-reviewed research. Every figure below is an estimate, not a measurement, and every assumption is stated openly so the numbers can be checked and challenged.

1

The estimation pipeline

  1. Words → tokens. Prompt word count × {{ tokens_per_word }} tokens/word (modern sub-word tokenisers average 1.2–1.5×).
  2. Input vs output. Output tokens are estimated at {{ output_ratio }}× the input count. Inference has two phases — parallel prefill of the prompt and sequential decode of the response; decode dominates energy, so each input token is weighted at only {{ input_energy_weight }}× a decode token.
  3. Tokens → energy. Effective tokens × the model's Wh-per-token figure, then multiplied by a data-centre overhead (PUE) of {{ pue }}.
  4. Energy → CO₂. Energy (kWh) × the grid carbon intensity of the selected region (kg CO₂e/kWh).

Uncertainty (±{{ combined_uncertainty }}%)

We never present a single number as exact. The estimate is the product of several uncertain factors whose relative uncertainties are combined in quadrature. The energy-per-token term dominates because vendors do not publish per-token energy for proprietary models.

Primary sources

  • Faiz et al. (2024) — LLMCarbon: operational + embodied carbon modelling.
